Case No. 004 — B2B AI/ML Product

Data Validation Platform

A B2B AI/ML product sold to other lenders — validating loan documents and borrower data at scale through classification, extraction, rules, and human-in-the-loop review.

The Problem

Lending partners needed to trust the loan documents and borrower data flowing through their pipelines, but validating that data manually didn't scale — not at the volume of loan documents moving through the industry, and not at the accuracy bar compliance required. Document quality varied widely by source, formats were inconsistent, and any validation product sold externally had to meet another company's compliance standard, not just an internal one.

This wasn't an internal tool — it was a product built to be sold to other lending companies, which raised the bar on reliability, auditability, and consistency beyond what an in-house-only system would need.

What Made It Hard

Data Quality

Incoming documents varied widely in format, scan quality, and structure across partners, which meant the classification and extraction models had to hold up against real-world mess, not clean sample data.

Accuracy Requirements

Because the output fed directly into partners' own loan decisions, the accuracy bar was set by compliance risk, not convenience — every extraction had to be trustworthy enough to reduce, not just relocate, manual review.

Scale

Processing millions of documents across hundreds of thousands of loans a month meant the pipeline had to hold its accuracy and latency under real production volume, not just in testing.

Compliance

As an externally sold product, the validation logic and audit trail had to satisfy each partner's own compliance function, not just internal standards — raising the bar on traceability throughout the pipeline.

Beyond the Numbers

At 92% accuracy and this volume, the platform eliminated manual review as the default path — human review became the exception, reserved for the cases the model itself flagged as uncertain, rather than a step every document had to pass through. That shift in where human effort gets spent is what made the product viable to sell externally, not just run internally.
